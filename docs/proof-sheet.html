SpamGuard 6.27.3 · test results, 5 November 2026

Does SpamGuard hold back your mesh? We measured it.

A made-up three-hour attack, ten different random runs, and three days of real traffic replayed. What got stopped, what got caught by mistake, and how to rerun it yourself.

96.6%of about 11,760 test spam messages stopped (every run between 96.4% and 96.9%)
2.7%of ordinary messages held by mistake during the attack hours themselves (35 of 1,282 in three runs, new people included)
0.8%of regulars' messages held across whole runs, which include six calm hours first (0.6% to 1.3% per run)
2 of 23messages marked fine held when three days of real traffic were replayed (Balanced setting)

The test is built to be harsher than a normal day: many kinds of spam at once, overlapping, while ordinary chat carries on. Nothing in it comes from a real person.

And on real traffic

Three days of logs from a live UK repeater were replayed on the same code, using messages the operator had marked as spam or fine.

2 of 23

messages marked fine were held at the Balanced setting: a link to a public forum and a bot's reply in a test channel. The Strict setting held 6, which is why it is not the default.

Not new people

Neither of those two was a newcomer. Only messages the operator marked were checked. In the test, new people who arrive through a repeater an attack is also using can be held, so that case stays on the watch list.

First minutes

Every spam message that got through in the newest log arrived in the first four minutes of a brand-new campaign, before there was enough evidence to build a block.

What keeps ordinary people safe

People it knows go first

Anyone SpamGuard has heard chatting normally is exempt from its repeater, route and lockdown blocks.

Blocks expire

Automatic blocks time out (two hours by default), so a mistake clears itself and a quiet repeater is not blocked for good.

Watch first

Monitor mode records what it would do without holding anything. You see the evidence before you switch Protect on.

Your rules win

SpamGuard only adds rules to openHop. It can be paused, and its rules removed, whenever you want.

What we do not claim

The test uses made-up traffic, so real mesh behaviour can differ. The first few minutes of a new campaign can get through. Some ordinary greetings that many people send can be held as copies. Spammers adapt, so the numbers will move. Attack methods are not published here. Found a way round it? Report it privately using SECURITY.md in the repository.

Rerun it yourself

The test ships with the code and uses openHop's own rule engine to judge every message. It takes under a minute.

SEED=7 python3 tests/redteam.py

Change the seed to get a different random run. It passes when at least 90% of spam is stopped and at most 3% of regulars are held.
