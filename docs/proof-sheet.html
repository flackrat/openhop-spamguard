SpamGuard 6.44.0 · test results, 21 November 2026

Does SpamGuard hold back your mesh? We measured it.

A made-up three-hour attack, ten different random runs, and six days of real traffic replayed. What got stopped, what got caught by mistake, and how to rerun it yourself.

98.1%of about 22,000 test spam messages stopped (every run between 97.6% and 98.4%)
4.8%of ordinary messages held by mistake during the attack hours themselves (60 of 1,261 in three runs, new people included)
1.1%of regulars' messages held across whole runs, which include six calm hours first (0.4% to 1.7% per run)
3 of 136messages marked fine held in six days of real traffic (Balanced), leaving out one news bot before it was trusted (17 more)

The test is built to be harsher than a normal day: many kinds of spam at once, overlapping, while ordinary chat carries on. It is harsher than the last sheet (5 November), so the attack-hours figure is higher: lockdowns now come back while a flood lasts, and those hold new people too. Nothing in it comes from a real person.

And on real traffic

Six days of logs from a live UK repeater (about 3,150 channel messages) were replayed on the same code, using messages the operator had marked as spam (69) or fine (153).

3 of 136

other messages marked fine were held at the Balanced setting. Automatic blocks expire, so even these clear on their own.

One bot, 17

A news bot posting in bursts was held before the operator trusted it. One tap of Trust lets it through from then on, on its own channel.

55 of 69

marked spam was caught, with no marks taught in advance. On a live install each mark you make sharpens what comes next.

What keeps ordinary people safe

People it knows go first

Anyone SpamGuard has heard chatting normally is exempt from its repeater, route and lockdown blocks.

Blocks expire

Automatic blocks time out (two hours by default), so a mistake clears itself and a quiet repeater is not blocked for good.

Watch first

Monitor mode records what it would do without holding anything. You see the evidence before you switch Protect on.

Your rules win

SpamGuard only adds rules to openHop. It can be paused, and its rules removed, whenever you want.

What we do not claim

The test uses made-up traffic, so real mesh behaviour can differ. The first few minutes of a new campaign can get through. Some ordinary greetings that many people send can be held as copies. Spammers adapt, so the numbers will move. Attack methods are not published here. Found a way round it? Report it privately using SECURITY.md in the repository.

Rerun it yourself

The test ships with the code and uses openHop's own rule engine to judge every message. It takes under a minute.

SEED=7 python3 tests/redteam.py

Change the seed to get a different random run. It passes when at least 90% of spam is stopped and at most 3% of regulars are held.
