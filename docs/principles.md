# Principles

A repeater is shared. Anything it refuses to pass on disappears for everyone downstream, and they can't see
why. So SpamGuard holds itself to these principles, and each one is something it does, not just something it
says.

SpamGuard protects the ability to communicate. It doesn't decide which opinions deserve to be heard.

| Principle | What SpamGuard does |
|---|---|
| **Behaviour, not views** | It judges how messages are sent: copies under many names, floods, made-up names. A political, religious or unpopular message is never spam because of what it says. There's no "block this person" button. |
| **Least restriction that works** | It holds further copies, not the person. Holds are short and expire once the spam stops. Bot-safe channels let bots run while still stopping spam. |
| **Suspicion isn't proof** | Places on the map say how sure they are. A shared repeater or a similar time is shown as a clue, never as proof that two senders are connected. Names SpamGuard knows get the benefit of the doubt. |
| **Explained** | Every held message says why, and for spam that got through it gives the likely reason, using the settings at the time. |
| **Reversible** | Answers can be taken back. "Not spam" says a message was fine without trusting the name for good; trusting a name is a separate, deliberate choice. |
| **Private** | Everything stays on the repeater. Names in shared files are scrambled, private channels are treated like people's names, and channel keys are never exported. Counts are kept rather than histories where that's enough. |
| **Accountable** | Setting changes are listed with who made them: you, a profile, or SpamGuard itself. Suggestions are kept apart from what you decide. |
| **Your repeater, your call** | Nothing is decided centrally. You choose the level, the channels, the experiments, and every block you add. |
| **No naming and shaming** | Holding someone's traffic doesn't label them a bad person. Nothing is published about who was held. |
| **Careful with new powers** | Anything new that can hold messages starts as an experiment, off by default, tested on real labelled traffic first. |

## Before adding a feature that can hold messages or identify senders

1. What does it protect, and what innocent traffic could it affect?
2. What evidence does it rely on, and how sure can that evidence be?
3. Can you see why it acted, and undo it?
4. How long does it keep what it learns?
5. Could it be used to silence a point of view?
6. Is there a gentler way to get the same protection?
